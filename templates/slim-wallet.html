Slim Wallet

A simple money-clip style wallet for up to four cards and cash. Measures in at just over 4mm thick.

Background

It's always when I'm wearing shorts in the summer that I realize how bulky my wallet is. In addition to this feeling, my cork wallet began falling apart last summer (2025), and so it was time for an upgrade. Like most people, I barely use most of the cards in my wallet. I took pictures/downloaded apps for all of my insurance cards and decided to stop carrying them around. In the end, I really only need my driver's license, credit card, and debit card.

I like the slim wallets that have become popular in the past several years. My primary complaint with the design of several of them is that they sandwich all your cards in one solid block between two plates of material. This feels like a loss of functionality over my giant wallet, where each card has its own spot, making it easy to select and grab a particular card at a glance. Additionally, the use of two external plates to contain the cards in the middle feels excessive. If the goal is to reduce bulk, why not place one plate in the middle of the stack of cards and fasten them to the outside of that?

Requirements

  1. The wallet design will prioritize lack of bulk, primarily by reducing thickness.
  2. The wallet must have unique poisitions for different cards, allowing the user to select and grab the right card at a glance.
  3. The wallet will not employ two outer covers to enclose its contents.
  4. The wallet will be made of durable materials and will be repairable.

Design

The design of the wallet is incredibly simple. The cards are stacked front to back and staggered in height such that each card pokes out of the stack by 10mm. This makes it easy to visually identify and select a card.

Most of the wallet is the backplate, which is formed from 1mm thick 5052 aluminum sheet. The backplate is the base on which the stack of cards sits, and has a flange on the left and right sides which are bent upward to prevent lateral movement of the cards. There are three risers, each of which supports the bottom edge of a card. The three risers are stacked on top of each other and riveted to the backplate with a solid aluminum rivet. The two lower risers are 0.8mm thick, and each support one card. The top riser is 1.5mm thick, and supports two cards.

There is a slight semicircle cut into the backplate on the left hand side which allows the user to easily push the cards away from the the backplate, making it easy to grab an individual card with the opposite hand.

To keep the card stack secured to the backplate, a spring steel clip applies pressure to the front of the card stack. Being mostly a wide flat piece, this clip can take many decorative shapes, and I have written some software to create different clips from images. This software is detailed here.

The wallet can be doubled up if desired to hold 8 cards + cash, but at that point the wallet would be around a centimeter thick, and isn't necessary for my needs.

Assembly

Each piece of the wallet is ordered from SendCutSend already laser cut to the proper shape. There are only three steps to assemble.

  1. Bend the two flanges on the backplate using a bench vise with press brake attachment.
  2. Bend the decorative clip into shape using a 3D printed punch/die in a bench vise.
  3. Stack the three risers on the back plate, put the clip on top, and fix the assembly together with two aluminum rivets.
